Twelve Olympians

The Heavens

Zeus / Jupiter

King of the gods; sky, thunder, and law.

King of Olympus after overthrowing his father Cronus, who had swallowed each of his children at birth. Zeus escaped that fate, freed his siblings, and led the Olympians to victory in the Titanomachy. He rules the sky and enforces oaths and hospitality, hurling thunderbolts forged by the Cyclopes at those who break them.

Hera / Juno

Queen of the gods; marriage and the family.

Zeus's wife and sister, and queen of the gods, Hera guards marriage and the sanctity of the household. Fiercely loyal and just as fiercely jealous, she pursued Zeus's mortal lovers and their children across the myths, from Heracles to Io, protecting her own status above all.

Sea & Harvest

Poseidon / Neptune

The sea, earthquakes, and horses.

Brother to Zeus and ruler of the seas, Poseidon can calm the waves or summon storms and earthquakes with a strike of his trident. He contested Athens with Athena and lost, but fathered the winged horse Pegasus and countless sea creatures, and sailors prayed to him before every voyage.

Demeter / Ceres

Harvest, agriculture, and the growing seasons.

Goddess of the harvest and the grain, Demeter's grief for her daughter Persephone, taken by Hades to the underworld, once left the earth barren through winter. Her joy at Persephone's yearly return brings spring, tying the growing seasons directly to a mother's love.

Wisdom & War

Athena / Minerva

Wisdom, strategy, and crafts.

Born fully armed from Zeus's forehead after he swallowed her mother Metis, Athena is goddess of wisdom, strategy, and crafts. She won Athens by gifting the olive tree, championed clever heroes like Odysseus, and is rarely seen without her owl and her aegis shield.

Ares / Mars

War, and the brutal side of it.

God of war in its rawest, bloodiest form, Ares delights in the chaos of battle rather than the strategy behind it, which is why Athena regularly bests him. Feared more than loved by the other Olympians, his affair with Aphrodite produced children who would themselves come to embody love and strife.

Forge & Love

Hephaestus / Vulcan

Fire, the forge, and craftsmanship.

Lame from birth and thrown from Olympus by an ashamed Hera, Hephaestus became the gods' master smith anyway, forging Zeus's thunderbolts, Achilles' armour, and automatons of gold. Married to Aphrodite, he built his forge beneath volcanoes, where the Cyclopes worked his bellows.

Aphrodite / Venus

Love, beauty, and desire.

Goddess of love, beauty, and desire, Aphrodite was born from sea-foam off Cyprus. Married to Hephaestus but drawn to Ares, her golden apple to Paris in the judgment of the goddesses set off the chain of events that led to the Trojan War.

Light & Wild

Apollo / Apollo

Sun, music, and prophecy.

God of the sun, music, poetry, and prophecy, Apollo speaks through the oracle at Delphi, which he claimed by slaying the serpent Python. Twin brother to Artemis, he is the ideal of youthful beauty and reason among the Olympians, as comfortable with a lyre as with a bow.

Artemis / Diana

The hunt, the moon, and the wilderness.

Goddess of the hunt, the moon, and the wilderness, Artemis was born first and helped deliver her twin brother Apollo. Fiercely protective of her chastity and her wild places, she turned the hunter Actaeon into a stag for glimpsing her bathing, and roams the forests with a retinue of nymphs.

Roads & Revelry

Hermes / Mercury

Messenger of the gods; travel and trade.

Messenger of the gods and guide of travellers, Hermes moves between Olympus, earth, and the underworld on winged sandals. Clever from birth — he stole Apollo's cattle as an infant and invented the lyre from a tortoise shell to make amends — he is also the god of thieves, merchants, and boundaries.

Dionysus / Bacchus

Wine, festivity, and ecstatic release.

God of wine, festivity, and ecstatic release, Dionysus was born mortal and reborn divine after Zeus rescued him from his dying mother Semele. His followers, the maenads, could be driven to frenzy in his name, and his rites promised transformation — for better or worse — to anyone who joined them.
